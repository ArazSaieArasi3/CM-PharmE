# Applications and Boundaries

CM-PharmE is intended to serve as a conceptual reference before implementation-specific architecture, data integration, standards conformance, or software design. The model provides a shared semantic layer through which organizational, collaborative, operational, governance, and digital concerns can be related and traced.

## Application pathways

### Governance and compliance reasoning

The model can be used to identify regulatory authorities, oversight relations, governance policies, compliance requirements, risk-related activities, and the operational constructs they constrain. The result is a conceptual governance trace rather than evidence of legal conformance.

### Requirements engineering

Actors, roles, processes, obligations, capabilities, and digital dependencies can be used as sources for candidate functional, non-functional, integration, data, and governance requirements. Requirements must subsequently be validated through established requirements-engineering practices.

### Interoperability-oriented analysis

CM-PharmE can provide a higher-level semantic context for implementation-specific mappings. Standards such as HL7 FHIR and ISO IDMP may be mapped to relevant concepts where appropriate, but CM-PharmE itself does not establish conformance with those standards.

### Reference-architecture interpretation

The five domains can help structure questions about organizational alignment, ecosystem collaboration, process traceability, governance, and digital enablement. They are conceptual domains, not a complete reference-architecture specification.

### Domain-driven design and software architecture

The model may expose candidate responsibility boundaries and semantic clusters that can inform later DDD analysis. The five CM-PharmE domains must not be treated as predefined bounded contexts, services, or microservices without additional domain and organizational analysis.

### Knowledge graphs and computational use

The formal ontology can support graph-based exploration, executable competency questions, instance validation, and later reasoning over representative ecosystem data. Those capabilities require explicit computational evaluation and suitable data mappings.

## Practical use workflow

A concise repository-oriented workflow adapted from the journal manuscript is:

1. Establish a bounded ecosystem and analysis scope.
2. Map organizations and contextual roles.
3. Map capabilities and strategic concerns.
4. Trace collaborative arrangements and relators.
5. Map business and clinical processes and participation.
6. Relate governance and compliance constraints to affected constructs.
7. Identify digital enablers and information dependencies.
8. Add standards-specific mappings only where required.
9. Derive downstream requirements and architecture concerns.
10. Record extension, legacy-mapping, and transition gaps rather than prematurely changing the core model.

## Evidential boundary

Current evidence supports conceptual traceability, representational plausibility, and ontology-informed structural discipline within the examined scope. It does not yet demonstrate:

- operational adoption;
- implementation feasibility or transition cost;
- measured organizational benefit;
- universal pharmaceutical-domain completeness;
- technical interoperability;
- multi-context extension performance;
- full computational consistency.

These boundaries are retained deliberately so that later repository evidence can strengthen individual claims without rewriting the historical record.
