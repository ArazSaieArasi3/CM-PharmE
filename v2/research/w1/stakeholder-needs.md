# W1 Stakeholder and Unmet-Information-Need Map

## Purpose
Identify concrete pharmaceutical-ecosystem decision/information needs before expanding the ontology. Needs are grounded in W1 application evidence and are not inferred solely from CM-PharmE 1.x.

| Stakeholder / role | Concrete decision or information need | Required semantic objects/relations | Evidence anchors | V2 implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medicines regulators / national competent authorities | Detect, assess and coordinate medicine shortages; understand supply, demand, availability, criticality and cross-jurisdiction effects. | Medicinal Product, Critical Medicine, Shortage, Supply, Demand, Availability, Jurisdiction, MAH, Site, Alternative Product/Site | W1-S03, S04 | Shortage/resilience semantics and jurisdiction are first-class cross-domain concerns. |
| Marketing authorisation holders | Report shortages, maintain product/site information, assess supply vulnerabilities, alternatives, stocks and prevention plans. | MAH, Product, Authorisation, Manufacturing Site, Supplier, Capacity/Availability observation, Shortage Prevention Action | W1-S03, S04 | Need explicit organizational roles, product/site identity and event/observation provenance. |
| Manufacturers / API manufacturers | Understand manufacturing scope, site compliance, dependencies, alternative production sites and disruption exposure. | Organization, Facility/Site, Manufacturing Activity, API/Substance, Product, GMP Status, Supplier/Dependency | W1-S01, S05, S16 | Facility/site must be distinct from organization and geospatially representable. |
| Wholesalers / distributors / logistics actors | Understand distribution authorisations, routes, storage/distribution dependencies and continuity of supply. | Distributor, WDA/GDP, Warehouse, Route, Shipment, Product, Jurisdiction, Dependency | W1-S05, S03 | Supply/logistics extension should model distribution roles and dependencies without overloading Core. |
| Pharmacies / healthcare providers | Know whether medicines are available, suitable alternatives exist, and which shortages/access constraints affect care. | Product, Availability, Shortage, Alternative, Facility, Region, Time | W1-S01, S03, S07 | Availability/access should be modeled as observations/situations with time and place. |
| Public-health / crisis-preparedness authorities | Identify critical medicines, vulnerable nodes and geographic concentration; evaluate stockpiling, alternate supply and crisis scenarios. | Criticality, Vulnerability, Site, Supplier, Geography, Stockpile, Capacity, Disruption, Recovery | W1-S04, S16, S18 | Supports Risk/Resilience extension and network/geospatial analytics. |
| Clinical-trial sponsors / collaborators | Discover trial-site networks, geographic coverage, intervention/product links, collaborator structures and recruitment footprint. | Study, Sponsor, Collaborator, Intervention, Product/Substance, Facility, Location, Country | W1-S06 | Clinical/Trial extension can test cross-source organization/site/product semantics. |
| Pharmacovigilance teams / regulators | Integrate adverse-event/safety evidence across products, reports, signals and sources while preserving provenance and access boundaries. | Product, Substance, Adverse Event, Safety Report, Signal, Reporter/Source, Evidence, Provenance | W1-S08, S11, S12 | Safety extension should remain modular; provenance and source identity belong in reusable evidence semantics. |
| Payers / reimbursement bodies | Compare reimbursement, access, utilisation and geographic/temporal variation without conflating price, coverage and availability. | Payer, Reimbursement Decision/Record, Product, Price/Amount, Coverage, Region, Time | W1-S07 | Market-access/reimbursement is a candidate extension and analytical use-case family. |
| Researchers / data stewards | Integrate heterogeneous product, organisation, facility, trial, regulatory, safety and access data with traceable mappings and identifiers. | Dataset, Record, Identifier, Mapping, Assertion, Evidence, Provenance, Entity Resolution | W1-S02, S05, S06, S08 | Provenance, identifiers and mapping semantics are foundational infrastructure concerns. |

| Pharmaceutical financiers / investors | Potentially assess ecosystem dependencies, asset/site concentration, counterparty exposure and financing relationships. | Organization, Financing Arrangement, Dependency, Asset/Site, Risk | Exploratory only | Retain as future opportunity until W2 finds legally reusable evidence. Do not place finance semantics in Core based on W1 alone. |

## Cross-cutting unmet needs
1. **Identity reconciliation:** the same organization, product, substance or site appears under different identifiers and names across sources.
2. **Organization–site distinction:** regulatory evidence often concerns physical sites while corporate data concerns organizations; conflating them damages geospatial and risk analysis.
3. **Temporal validity:** authorisations, shortages, trial status, supply availability and safety evidence change over time.
4. **Jurisdiction:** the meaning/status of a product, authorisation, facility or shortage is jurisdiction-dependent.
5. **Evidence/provenance:** analytical results need traceability from assertion to dataset/source/record.
6. **Cross-domain dependency:** shortages and resilience depend on relations spanning product, organization, site, supply, regulatory and geography.
7. **Machine-queryable interoperability:** stakeholders need reusable semantics across relational, RDF/KG and API representations.

## W1 conclusion
The strongest ecosystem-level unmet-information needs are not simply “more pharmaceutical data.” They are **cross-source identity, cross-domain dependency, geospatial/jurisdiction context, temporal status and provenance-aware integration**. These should drive V2 concept discovery and later evaluation.
