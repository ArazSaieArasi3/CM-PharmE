# W7-E9 Manuscript Method Notes — Prospective Structured Expert Evaluation

CM-PharmE 2.0 uses a prospective structured expert-review design that is analytically separated from logical, data, held-out and application evaluation. Before collecting any expert response, the repository freezes: participant eligibility and recruitment criteria, the review instrument, scoring scales, confidence ratings, qualitative finding categories, red-flag rules, anonymization/data-governance procedures and the analysis plan.

The planned sample is purposive rather than statistically representative. Recruitment targets 6–12 completed reviews with representation from both pharmaceutical-domain and ontology/conceptual-modeling expertise, preferably at least two eligible participants in each stratum. Eligibility is established before response analysis.

The instrument evaluates clarity, semantic adequacy, domain relevance, protected identity distinctions, module boundaries, provenance semantics, intended-task usefulness, missing concepts/relations and inappropriate distinctions. Rating items use a five-point ordinal scale and record item-level confidence. Open comments are retained for qualitative analysis.

Primary reporting is dimension-specific: response counts, medians, interquartile ranges, category distributions, favorable ratings (4–5), critical ratings (1–2), confidence and traceable qualitative findings. No single composite expert score is used. Predefined red flags trigger documented adjudication rather than automatic model changes.

Expert results may be reported only after real eligible participants complete the frozen instrument. Until then, the repository/manuscript records the method as protocol-frozen and recruitment-ready; no sample characteristics, ratings or expert conclusions are inferred or reconstructed.
